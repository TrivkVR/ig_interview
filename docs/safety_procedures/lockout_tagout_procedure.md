# Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) Procedure SOP-SAF-001

[[PAGE 1]]

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose
This procedure establishes the minimum requirements for isolating hazardous energy before any servicing or maintenance of machines and equipment. It prevents unexpected energization, start-up or release of stored energy that could injure personnel.

### 1.2 Scope
This procedure applies to all employees and contractors who service or maintain presses, conveyors, CNC machining centers, injection molding machines and packaging lines in Plants 1 and 2. Normal production operations and minor tool changes covered by alternate guarding are excluded.

## 2. Roles and Responsibilities

### 2.1 Authorized Employees
Authorized employees are trained and certified to apply locks and tags. Each authorized employee must hold a personal red lock with a unique key and must never share it. Authorized employees perform the lockout steps in Section 3.

### 2.2 Affected Employees
Affected employees operate or work near locked-out equipment. They must never attempt to start, energize or use a machine bearing a lock or tag.

[[PAGE 2]]

### 2.3 Floor Supervisors
Floor supervisors verify that the LOTO permit is complete before work begins, confirm that all group lock boxes are populated, and ensure shift handover includes the status of any locks still in place.

## 3. Lockout Sequence

### 3.1 Preparation and Shutdown
Identify all energy sources using the machine-specific energy control card posted on the equipment. Notify affected employees. Shut the machine down using the normal stop procedure. Do not use the emergency stop as a routine shutdown method.

### 3.2 Isolation and Lock Application
Operate each energy-isolating device (electrical disconnect, pneumatic valve, hydraulic valve) to the safe or OFF position. Apply a personal lock and a danger tag to each device. The tag must show the employee name, date and department.

### 3.3 Stored Energy Release
Relieve or restrain all stored energy. Bleed pneumatic and hydraulic lines to zero pressure, block raised press rams with safety blocks, and discharge capacitors. Allow thermal energy in heaters and barrels to dissipate to below 50 degrees C.

[[PAGE 3]]

### 3.4 Verification of Isolation
Attempt a normal start from the operator control station to verify that the machine does not operate. Return controls to OFF afterward. Use a calibrated voltmeter on electrical systems to confirm zero energy (test-before-touch: test the meter on a known live source, test the circuit, re-test the meter).

## 4. Lock Removal and Restoring Equipment

### 4.1 Pre-Removal Checks
Before removing locks, inspect the work area to confirm that tools and spare parts are removed, guards are reinstalled and all personnel are clear. Notify affected employees that the machine will be re-energized.

### 4.2 Removal of a Lock by Another Person
Only the employee who applied a lock may remove it. If that employee is unavailable, the Safety Manager may authorize removal after making every reasonable effort to contact the employee and after confirming the employee is not on site. The employee must be informed before resuming work.

## 5. Group Lockout and Shift Change

### 5.1 Group Lockout
When several employees work on one machine, each applies a personal lock to the group lock box. The authorized lead holds the key to the box locked with the isolation devices. The box cannot be opened until every personal lock is removed.

### 5.2 Shift Change
Outgoing and incoming employees must meet at the machine. The incoming employee applies a personal lock before the outgoing employee removes theirs, so that no gap in protection occurs.
