# Statistical Process Control and Capability Standard QC-STD-205

[[PAGE 1]]

## 1. Control Charts

### 1.1 Chart Selection
Use X-bar and R charts for subgroup sizes of 2 to 9, individuals and moving range charts for subgroup size 1, and p charts for fraction nonconforming data. Take subgroups of 5 consecutive parts every 30 minutes for machined features.

### 1.2 Control Limits
Calculate control limits at plus and minus 3 sigma from the center line using at least 25 subgroups. Do not use specification limits as control limits. Recalculate only after a verified process change.

## 2. Out-of-Control Signals

### 2.1 Detection Rules
Investigate when any point lies beyond a control limit, 7 consecutive points fall on one side of the center line, 6 consecutive points trend up or down, or 2 of 3 points are beyond 2 sigma on the same side.

### 2.2 Response Actions
When a signal occurs, stop the process, contain parts produced since the last good check, notify the quality engineer, identify the assignable cause and record the action on the chart.

## 3. Process Capability

### 3.1 Cp and Cpk Requirements
Capability is computed on a stable process using at least 50 measurements. The minimum Cpk is 1.33 for standard characteristics, and 1.67 for safety or critical characteristics. New processes require Ppk of at least 1.67 at launch.

### 3.2 Actions for Low Capability
If Cpk is below the requirement, implement 100 percent inspection of the characteristic until improved, and open a corrective action. Do not adjust limits to hide a capability gap.

## 4. Measurement System Analysis

### 4.1 Gauge R&R Acceptance
Perform gauge R&R with 10 parts, 3 operators and 3 trials. Total gauge R&R below 10 percent of tolerance is acceptable, 10 to 30 percent is conditional, and over 30 percent is unacceptable.

### 4.2 Calibration Intervals
Calibrate gauges annually unless otherwise stated, traceable to national standards. Out-of-tolerance finds trigger review of all product measured since the last good calibration.
