# Incoming Inspection and Sampling Standard QC-STD-101

[[PAGE 1]]

## 1. Scope and Definitions

### 1.1 Scope
This standard defines how purchased raw material and components are inspected on receipt, including sampling plans and disposition rules.

### 1.2 Definitions
Acceptable Quality Level (AQL) is the maximum percent nonconforming considered acceptable. A lot is a quantity of product from a single supplier batch. A critical defect may cause injury or non-compliance, a major defect affects function, and a minor defect affects appearance only.

## 2. Sampling Plans

### 2.1 AQL Levels
Use ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 General Inspection Level II, with AQL 0.065 for critical characteristics, 1.0 for major, and 2.5 for minor defects. Switch to tightened inspection after 2 of 5 consecutive lots are rejected.

### 2.2 Sample Size Selection
For a lot of 501 to 1200 units, the sample size code is J, which requires a sample of 80 units. Draw samples randomly across the lot, covering all boxes and pallets.

## 3. Inspection Methods

### 3.1 Dimensional Inspection
Measure critical dimensions with calibrated calipers, micrometers or CMM. Gauges must be within their calibration interval. Record measured values, not just pass or fail.

### 3.2 Visual and Documentation Checks
Check packaging damage, labeling, lot number traceability, and the certificate of conformance and material test report. Material certificates must match the purchase order specification.

## 4. Nonconforming Material

### 4.1 Rejection and Quarantine
Reject the lot when defects exceed the accept number. Tag the material with a red NONCONFORMING label and move it to the quarantine cage within 4 hours.

### 4.2 Supplier Corrective Action
Issue a Supplier Corrective Action Request (SCAR) within 2 business days. The supplier must respond with root cause and corrective action within 10 business days.
